# PersonaGraph  - Most Valuable Use Cases

## What PersonaGraph Is

PersonaGraph is a graph-backed scenario simulation workbench. It ingests source material, derives an ontology and actor graph, generates a realistic cast of personas, runs a multi-agent social simulation, produces an evidence-grounded report, and lets the analyst interrogate both the report and the simulated actors.

The core workflow is: **Graph -> Setup -> Simulate -> Report -> Interact**.

This is not a chatbot and not a dashboard. It is a scenario construction engine with a synthetic actor ecosystem and traceable reporting.

---

## Top Use Cases

### 1. Brand Launch and Campaign Reaction Testing

**Who it serves:** Brand strategy teams, agencies, integrated marketing teams.

**The problem:** Teams need to anticipate public reaction before committing budget to a campaign, product launch, or brand repositioning.

**How PersonaGraph solves it:**

- Upload a campaign brief or brand strategy document.
- The platform builds an actor graph of consumers, media, influencers, competitors, and institutional stakeholders.
- The simulation shows how different cohorts react, amplify, criticize, or ignore the campaign over time.
- The report highlights likely fault lines, supporting voices, and opposing narratives.
- The analyst can interview individual personas to pressure-test messaging.

**Example:** Upload a luxury brand's seasonal strategy brief and simulate how heritage loyalists, Gen Z audiences, fashion media, and competitor brands react to a premiumization shift.

---

### 2. Reputation and Crisis Stress Testing

**Who it serves:** Communications teams, crisis managers, executive offices.

**The problem:** When a controversial event is anticipated or underway, teams need to understand who will amplify the story, what narratives will dominate, and where response plans will fail.

**How PersonaGraph solves it:**

- Ingest a scenario brief describing the crisis or controversy.
- The graph maps critics, advocates, media outlets, regulators, consumer groups, and competitors.
- The simulation models narrative spread, counter-reactions, and coalition dynamics over rounds.
- The report surfaces risk clusters, escalation paths, and strategic implications.
- Follow-up interaction lets the analyst test response options against the simulated stakeholders.

**Example:** Model how sustainability critics, investigative journalists, advocacy groups, and loyal customers respond to a supply-chain labor controversy.

---

### 3. Market Entry and Geographic Expansion Planning

**Who it serves:** Corporate strategy, regional expansion teams, market intelligence analysts.

**The problem:** Entering a new market involves anticipating how local competitors, media, regulators, cultural dynamics, and consumer segments will respond.

**How PersonaGraph solves it:**

- Ingest research briefs covering the target market's competitive landscape, institutions, and consumer dynamics.
- The graph captures locations, local competitors, institutions, media, and consumer segments.
- The simulation models friction points, resonance, and opposition across actor types.
- The report delivers a structured market-entry risk and opportunity assessment.

**Example:** Simulate how a fashion brand's entry into Tier-2 Chinese cities is received by local retailers, domestic competitors, social media influencers, and aspirational consumer cohorts.

---

### 4. Executive and Spokesperson Narrative Testing

**Who it serves:** Executive communications, PR teams, advisory firms.

**The problem:** Before a CEO announcement, public statement, or leadership change, teams need to anticipate how different audiences will interpret and react to the narrative.

**How PersonaGraph solves it:**

- The graph models media, analysts, employees, investors, critics, and advocates around the executive.
- The simulation shows how each stakeholder group reacts to the proposed narrative.
- The interact step lets the analyst directly question simulated stakeholders to refine messaging.

**Example:** Test how financial media, long-time brand loyalists, and Gen Z consumers react to a new CEO's strategic vision statement.

---

### 5. Channel Strategy and Pricing Reaction Analysis

**Who it serves:** Product strategy, pricing teams, retail and commerce planners.

**The problem:** Shifts in distribution (DTC vs. wholesale, premium vs. off-price) and pricing affect different customer segments in conflicting ways.

**How PersonaGraph solves it:**

- Ingest a strategy document describing the proposed channel or pricing change.
- The graph maps shoppers, analysts, retail partners, purists, and deal-seekers.
- The simulation shows how different segments react to the tradeoff.
- The report highlights likely narrative splits, loyalty risk, and brand-perception shifts.

**Example:** Simulate how reducing off-price exposure and raising average prices affects heritage customers, deal-seeking shoppers, wholesale partners, and fashion analysts.

---

### 6. Strategic Briefing and Workshop Preparation

**Who it serves:** Internal insights teams, consultants, foresight teams, innovation labs.

**The problem:** Analysts need to rapidly build structured, evidence-backed scenario briefs for leadership workshops, strategy reviews, or client presentations.

**How PersonaGraph solves it:**

- The complete graph-to-report pipeline produces an inspectable, multi-section strategic brief in minutes.
- Each stage is transparent  - the analyst can verify the graph, review the cast, inspect simulation activity, and question the report.
- Outputs are grounded in graph evidence and simulation dynamics, not opaque model answers.

**Example:** Prepare a pre-mortem scenario brief for a board meeting by simulating how key stakeholders react to a proposed acquisition, partnership, or product pivot.

---

### 7. Public Affairs and Stakeholder Mapping

**Who it serves:** Policy teams, advocacy organizations, public-affairs consultancies.

**The problem:** Understanding how institutions, advocacy groups, critics, media, and affected populations will respond to a policy proposal or public-affairs campaign.

**How PersonaGraph solves it:**

- The graph naturally represents institutional actors, advocacy coalitions, media, regulators, and affected communities.
- The simulation models contested environments where narratives compete and coalitions form.
- The report and interaction steps let the analyst explore opposition dynamics and test communication strategies.

**Example:** Map and simulate stakeholder response to a sustainability regulation affecting global supply chains.

## What Makes These Use Cases Valuable

| Property | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Graph-grounded personas** | Simulated actors are derived from real source material, not generic templates. |
| **Multi-agent social simulation** | Reactions emerge from agent interaction, not single-prompt summarization. |
| **Transparent intermediate steps** | Every stage is inspectable  - graph, cast, simulation timeline, report sections. |
| **Evidence-backed reporting** | Reports cite graph statistics, runtime signals, and interview evidence. |
| **Interactive follow-up** | Analysts can interrogate both the report and individual personas after the simulation. |
